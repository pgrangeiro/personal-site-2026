When engineers transition into management, traditional wisdom tells them to put away their IDEs and step back entirely from code. "Your job is people, not pull requests," the old playbook says.

In the era of agentic software development, **this traditional advice is actively dangerous.**

Staying hands-on as a leader doesn't mean hogging critical path features or blocking team velocity. It means maintaining **tactical intuition**. Here is how I structure my weekly 10% technical allocation:

1. **Build internal dev-productivity tools:** Don't build product features. Build the PR evaluation harnesses, prompt regression tests, or CI sanity checks your team needs.
2. **Review AI-generated diffs:** Dedicate 1 hour every Tuesday reviewing pull requests created by coding agents to spot architectural drift early.
3. **Write Evals, Not Just Rules:** In an AI-augmented team, code quality is enforced through evaluation datasets rather than human linter rules.

> "If an engineering manager cannot run and evaluate their team's AI tooling, they are managing blindfolded in a high-speed vehicle."
